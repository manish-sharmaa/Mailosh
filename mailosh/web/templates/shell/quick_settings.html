{# Quick settings (spec §10): the gear's 280px popover — Appearance (Theme, Density, keyboard shortcuts) and Reading (mark-as-read delay, what to do after archiving), applied live and persisted through `POST /prefs` (`mailosh/web/prefs.py`). Reading pane and conversation view are §10's other two quick toggles and are deliberately absent: neither has anything to apply to in this phase (there is no reading pane, and the list is always conversation-grouped), and a control that persists a preference nothing reads is a promise the UI cannot keep — spec §3. Remote images and dark mail are §10's two reading controls whose consumers are the frame routes; they belong beside those, not here. --------------------------------------------------------------------- Two ways a control reaches the server, and why The three Appearance fields change what is already on screen, so they go through `app.js`'s `ui` store (`data-pref`): it writes ``'s dataset first and POSTs second, so the theme changes on the click rather than on the round trip, and reverts if the write did not land. The Reading fields change nothing on the current page — `mark_read_delay` and `auto_advance` are read by `mailosh/web/mail.py` on the *next* conversation it renders — so there is nothing to paint optimistically and no store entry to keep in sync. Those post themselves: `hx-post="/prefs"` on the input, `hx-trigger="change"`, `hx-swap="none"` against a route that answers `204` with no body. htmx sends a named input's own value, so one radio is one partial update, and the CSRF header comes from `hx-headers` on `` (`layouts/app.html`), inherited like every other htmx request in this app. No `hx-on:`, no `hx-vals='js:…'`, no `hx-trigger` filter — none of the three attribute forms the CSP's missing `'unsafe-eval'` would silently break. --------------------------------------------------------------------- Native controls, on purpose Each segmented group is a real `
` of ``, and the toggle is a real ``. That buys the entire radiogroup keyboard contract — one tab stop per group, arrows to move and choose, Space on the switch — plus correct screen reader semantics, from the platform rather than from JavaScript that would have to reimplement all of it. The inputs are visually replaced (`.segment`/`.switch` in styles/input.css), never hidden with `display: none`, so they stay focusable. `static/js/app.js` listens for `change` on `[data-pref]` and calls the `ui` store's `setPref()`, which writes ``'s dataset first and POSTs second — the theme changes on the click, not on the round trip. The same store method is what the palette's "Theme: dark" result calls, so both surfaces are one write. --------------------------------------------------------------------- A native ``, opened modally and pinned to the top right `showModal()` gives the focus trap, `Esc`, and a backdrop whose click is the only close-on-click-out a `` offers — `.quick::backdrop` is transparent, so it reads as a popover rather than a modal while still dismissing like one. `.quick`'s own `margin` is what puts it under the gear (see styles/input.css: a modal `` is centred by the UA's `margin: auto`, which Tailwind's preflight zeroes). No Alpine directive anywhere on this subtree, so there is no lone `x-show` for Alpine to skip for want of an `[x-data]` root, and no `x-show` losing to the `hidden` attribute — the two silent failures `fragments/offline.html` documents. Served-HTML state comes from `prefs`; after that the store owns it and re-syncs these controls whenever it changes, so a theme switched from the palette shows up here too. Rendered once by layouts/app.html, outside `#main`, for the same reason as the `?` overlay: every swap in this app replaces `#main` or `#list`, and the listeners bound to this dialog have to outlive them. #}
